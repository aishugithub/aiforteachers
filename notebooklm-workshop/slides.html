What is Gemini Notebook?

An AI that reads only what you give it

General chatbot: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini app

Answers from everything it has learned

  • May mix up syllabi and boards
  • Hard to check where a fact came from
  • Will answer even when it doesn't know
Gemini Notebook

Answers from your sources

  • Uses your textbook, notes, videos
  • Every answer has clickable citations
  • Says so when the sources don't cover the question

A free Google tool at notebooklm.google.com, also available as an Android and iOS app. Called NotebookLM until July 2026. It runs on Google's Gemini models.

Key idea: "source-grounded". Don't confuse Gemini Notebook with the Gemini chat app: the chat app is a general chatbot like ChatGPT; Gemini Notebook answers only from your sources. Researchers call this approach retrieval-augmented generation (RAG). Think of a very fast teaching assistant who has read only your books.

Beyond the classroom

Teachers also run the school: admin with AI

School rulebook notebook

Put every circular, the staff handbook, academic calendar and board guidelines in one shared notebook. Ask "What is the re-test rule?" and get the answer with the circular cited.

Staff meeting minutes

Upload the meeting recording. Get minutes, decisions and a who-does-what list, each linked to the moment it was said.

Question paper check

Upload the syllabus, blueprint and your draft paper. Ask which chapters are over- or under-tested and whether marks match the blueprint.

Class list follow-up

From an anonymised marks and attendance sheet: who needs remedial classes, who is below 75% attendance. Next slide.

Parent communication

PTM agendas, progress letters and reminders drafted from the circular itself, so dates and rules are right.

Reports & inspections

Turn event notes, club records and circulars into a monthly report, or check your files against an inspection checklist.

Rule of thumb: if the answer is in a document you have, put that document in a notebook.

This is the slide that usually gets the biggest reaction. Ask: how much time did you spend last month looking for a line in a circular? The "school rulebook notebook" is the easiest win: a head of department builds it once, shares it chat-only with all staff, and everyone gets cited answers. Meeting recordings: get consent before recording. Question paper check: ask "Map each question to a chapter and a Bloom's level, then total the marks per chapter" and compare with the blueprint.
Worked example · Your class list

Working with a student list, safely

  1. Anonymise. Keep roll numbers. Delete names, phone numbers, addresses, health notes.
  2. Upload the sheet as a Google Sheet or CSV file.
  3. Ask questions. Click citations to check the rows.
  4. Put names back yourself in Excel or Sheets, using the roll numbers.
Which students scored below 10 in either unit test? Show their attendance too.

Why it works

Grounded in research on AI and on learning

Fewer made-up facts

Answering from retrieved documents (retrieval-augmented generation) makes AI output more factual and traceable than answering from memory alone [1]. Hallucination is reduced, not removed [2].

Testing helps memory

Practice quizzes and flashcards use retrieval practice, one of the most effective study methods known [3, 4].

Words + visuals

Mind maps and videos pair words with images, which supports understanding and recall (dual coding) [5].

Listening as review

Audio overviews give students another way to meet the same content, useful for auditory learners and revision on the go.

[1] Lewis et al., Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks, NeurIPS 2020 · [2] Ji et al., Survey of Hallucination in Natural Language Generation, ACM Computing Surveys 2023 [3] Roediger & Karpicke, Test-Enhanced Learning, Psychological Science 2006 · [4] Dunlosky et al., Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques, PSPI 2013 · [5] Clark & Paivio, Dual Coding Theory and Education, Educ. Psych. Review 1991
Keep this short. The point: the features aren't gimmicks. Quizzes and flashcards are retrieval practice, which Dunlosky et al. rate as high-utility. Be honest that grounding reduces errors but doesn't eliminate them.

ChatGPT and Claude can also work from your uploaded files. The difference is the default: Gemini Notebook is built to stay inside your sources and prove it.

Metaphor for the room: Gemini Notebook is a librarian who has read only your files and always shows you the page. ChatGPT and Claude are brilliant all-rounders who know a lot, write well and can do maths on a spreadsheet, but you have to check their facts. Be fair: both tools are good, they're built for different jobs. Specialist teacher apps (MagicSchool, Khanmigo) sit in between: templates for lesson plans and rubrics, less control over sources.

Use it responsibly

Five rules before you use it with students

  • No identifiable student data. Roll numbers only: no names, phone numbers, health or family details. India's DPDP Act, 2023 applies.
  • Verify before you teach. Click the citations. It can still misread a source.
  • Respect copyright. Upload only material you are allowed to use.
  • Follow school policy and Google's age rules before sharing with students.
  • You stay the teacher. The AI drafts; your judgement decides.

AI prepares the draft. The teacher makes the lesson.

Pause here and take questions on privacy. Google says personal-account uploads are not used to train its models, and Workspace for Education has further protections, but the safest rule is never to upload anything identifying students. The same rule applies to ChatGPT, Claude and every other AI tool. For class lists: remove names, keep roll numbers, and match names back on your own computer.
