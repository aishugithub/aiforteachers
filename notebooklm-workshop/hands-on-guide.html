Before you start

The NotebookLM screen in three parts

Every notebook has the same three panels. On a phone they show as three tabs at the bottom.

SourcesLeft panel. The material you upload: PDFs, links, videos, notes. The AI answers only from these.
ChatMiddle panel. Ask questions about your sources. Every answer has numbered citations you can click to see the exact line.
StudioRight panel. One-click teaching material: audio and video overviews, mind maps, reports, quizzes, flashcards, slides.

Sources in → answers and materials out.

Part A · Follow the demo

Guided steps

Menu names change as Google updates the tool. If a button looks slightly different, look for the closest match in the same panel.

  1. Open NotebookLM and sign in

    • Go to notebooklm.google.com in Chrome, Edge or Safari.
    • Sign in with your Google account. A school Google Workspace account works if your school admin has switched NotebookLM on.
    • On a phone, you can install the NotebookLM app from the Play Store or App Store instead.

    You should see: a home page with a Create new button and some featured notebooks from Google.

  2. Create your first notebook

    • Click Create new. A window opens asking for sources. Close it for now or keep it open for the next step.
    • Click the title Untitled notebook at the top and rename it, for example Class 7 Science – Water Cycle.

    Tip: Make one notebook per chapter or unit. Small, focused notebooks give sharper answers than one huge notebook for the whole year.

  3. Add sources

    Click + Add in the Sources panel and try at least two of these:

    • Copied text: choose Copied text and paste the sample source from this page.
    • PDF: upload a textbook chapter. NCERT chapters are free to download from ncert.nic.in/textbook.php.
    • YouTube: paste the link to a public educational video that has captions.
    • Website: paste the link to any article, such as a Wikipedia page on the topic.
    • Google Drive: pick your own Google Docs or Slides lesson plans.

    You should see: each source listed on the left with a tick box. A short summary of all sources appears in the Chat panel.

  4. Read the summary and control which sources are used

    • Read the auto-generated summary and the suggested questions under it.
    • Click on a source name to open its source guide: a summary and key topics for that one file.
    • Untick a source's box to leave it out of answers. Tick it again to bring it back.

    Tip: Unticking is useful when you want an answer from the textbook only, not from the YouTube video.

  5. Ask questions and check citations

    Type these into the chat box, one at a time:

    List the 5 most important concepts in these sources, with a one-line explanation of each for a Class 7 student.
    What misconceptions do students commonly have about this topic? Use only the sources.
    • Hover over or click a small number in the answer. The exact line in the source opens on the left.
    • Now ask something that is not in your sources, e.g. Who won the 2011 Cricket World Cup? Notice how it tells you the sources don't cover it.

    Why this matters: you can verify every claim in seconds before it reaches your students.

  6. Set the chat style and output language

    • Click the settings / configure icon at the top of the Chat panel.
    • Try Learning guide: the AI asks the student guiding questions instead of giving the answer straight away.
    • Or choose Custom and type a role, for example: You are a patient Class 7 science tutor. Use simple English and one everyday example.
    • To get answers and materials in Tamil, Hindi or another language: open Settings → Output language in the top-right menu.

  7. Save useful answers as notes

    • Under any good answer, click Save to note. It appears in the Studio panel under Notes.
    • Open a note to edit it, or add your own note with + Add note.
    • A note can be turned into a source with Convert to source, so your own ideas become part of the notebook.

  8. Make a mind map

    • In Studio, click Mind Map.
    • Click on any branch to expand it. Click a node to ask the chat about that sub-topic.
    • Download it as an image to project in class or paste into a worksheet.

    Classroom idea: Show only the main branches, and ask students to predict what comes under each before you expand them.

  9. Create a study guide or other report

    • In Studio, click Reports. Pick Study guide, Briefing doc, FAQ, Timeline or one of the suggested formats.
    • Use the pencil / customise option to create your own format. Try the prompt below.
    Create a one-page revision sheet for Class 7 students: 8 key terms with simple definitions, 3 diagrams described in words, and 5 short-answer questions with answers at the end.

  10. Generate a quiz and flashcards

    • In Studio, click Quiz. Use the pencil icon to set the number of questions and difficulty, and add a focus, e.g. Focus on evaporation and condensation.
    • Click Flashcards the same way for revision cards.
    • Take the quiz yourself. Each wrong answer shows an explanation linked to the source.

    Check first: read every question before you use it in class. Remove any that are unclear or off-syllabus.

  11. Make an Audio Overview (the "podcast")

    • In Studio, click the pencil on Audio Overview to customise it before generating.
    • Pick a format: Deep dive (conversation), Brief (short), Critique or Debate. Pick a length and a language.
    • Paste instructions like the one below, then click Generate. It takes a few minutes; you can keep working meanwhile.
    • While it plays, try Interactive mode (Join): interrupt the two hosts and ask them a question with your microphone.
    The listeners are Class 7 students in Chennai. Explain in simple words, use one everyday example from Chennai weather, and end with three questions for the students to think about.

  12. Try Video Overview, Slide Deck and Infographic

    • Video Overview: a narrated slideshow of your sources. Choose a visual style and add instructions the same way as audio.
    • Slide Deck: ready-made presentation slides you can download and edit.
    • Infographic: a one-page visual summary for a notice board or worksheet.

    Tip: These take longer to generate. Start one, then carry on with the next step.

  13. Find new sources with Discover or Deep Research

    • In the Sources panel, use the search the web / Discover box. Type a topic, e.g. water conservation projects for school students in India.
    • Choose Fast research for a quick list or Deep research for a full report with sources.
    • Read the suggestions and import only the ones from sites you trust.

  14. Share the notebook

    • Click Share at the top right.
    • Add a colleague's email as Editor to plan together, or as Viewer to let them use it.
    • Where your account allows, choose Chat only so students can ask questions but not see or change the sources, or switch on Anyone with the link.

    Before sharing with students: follow your school's rules and Google's age requirements. Never put student personal data into a shared notebook.

Sample source to paste

An original short text written for this workshop. Copy it and add it as Copied text if you don't have your own material with you.

The Water Cycle – Class 7 Notes

The water on Earth is always moving. The same water has been going round and round for millions of years. This continuous movement of water between the land, the oceans and the air is called the water cycle.

Evaporation. The Sun heats water in oceans, lakes, rivers and even puddles. The water changes into invisible water vapour and rises into the air. Wet clothes drying on a terrace in Chennai is evaporation at work. Evaporation happens faster when it is hot, windy and dry.

Transpiration. Plants take up water through their roots and release water vapour through tiny pores in their leaves called stomata. A large tree can release hundreds of litres of water in a single day.

Condensation. As water vapour rises, it cools. Cool vapour turns back into tiny droplets of liquid water. Millions of these droplets together form clouds. The drops on the outside of a cold glass of water also come from condensation, not from water leaking through the glass.

Precipitation. When the droplets in a cloud join and become too heavy, they fall as rain, snow or hail. Tamil Nadu gets most of its rain from the north-east monsoon between October and December.

Collection. Rainwater flows into rivers, lakes and the sea, or soaks into the ground to become groundwater. Wells and borewells draw on this groundwater. Rainwater harvesting helps refill it.

Common misconceptions. Many students think clouds are made of water vapour or smoke. Clouds are actually made of liquid water droplets or ice crystals. Some students also think the Sun "sucks up" water; in fact the Sun only provides heat energy.

Why it matters. Only about 3% of Earth's water is fresh water, and most of that is frozen in ice. Saving water and harvesting rain protect the small share we can use.

Use it responsibly

Safe-use checklist

  • No personal data. Don't upload students' names, marks, phone numbers, health or family details. India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 applies to this data.
  • Verify before you teach. Click the citations. NotebookLM makes fewer mistakes because it uses your sources, but it can still misread them.
  • Respect copyright. Upload only material you are allowed to use: your own notes, free textbooks like NCERT, open resources.
  • Follow school policy and Google's age rules before sharing notebooks with students.
  • Keep your judgement. The AI drafts; you decide what is right for your class.
  • Tell students when material was AI-generated and teach them to check the sources too.
When you get stuck

Help & FAQ

Is NotebookLM free?

Yes, the basic version is free with any Google account. There are daily limits on chats and generated overviews. Paid plans (Google AI Pro and some Workspace plans) raise the limits. Check notebooklm.google for the current numbers.

How many sources can I add?

On the free plan, up to 50 sources per notebook at the time of writing, and each source can be very long (hundreds of pages). Paid plans allow more.

My YouTube link won't add.

The video must be public and have captions. Try another video, or paste the transcript as copied text.

My PDF gives poor answers.

Scanned PDFs (photos of pages) may not be read well. Use a PDF where you can select the text, or a clearer scan.

The answer is wrong or strange.

Click the citation to see what the source actually says. Rephrase the question, untick unrelated sources, or add a better source.

Can I get answers in Tamil or Hindi?

Yes. Ask in that language, or set Settings → Output language. Audio and video overviews can also be generated in many Indian languages.

Does Google use my uploads to train its AI?

Google states that NotebookLM does not use your sources or chats to train its models for personal accounts, and Workspace for Education accounts have added data protections. Still, avoid uploading anything confidential.

I can't see a feature shown in the workshop.

Google releases features gradually and renames buttons. Refresh the page, update the app, or check your school account settings with your admin.
